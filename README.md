# FirstCommit '26: Portable Heavy Metal Microfluidic Sensor

### What I built
I built a low-cost, zero-electricity portable microfluidic analytical device. It simultaneously detects four heavy metals (Lead, Mercury, Copper, and Zinc) directly from turbid, muddy groundwater within minutes, right in the field.

### Tech Stack (Materials & Reagents)
* **Hardware:** Translucent plastic casing, plastic macro-funnel, raw medical cotton, double-sided adhesive tape (4-hole manifold).
* **Substrate:** Whatman Grade 1 filter paper, hydrophobic wax coating (forming 4 isolated quadrants).
* **Chemicals:** Universal colourimetric indicator (Dithizone in ethanol), and four distinct pH buffers (1.5, 4.0, 5.5, and 9.5).

### How it solves the problem
It solves the two biggest points of failure for field diagnostics. First, the raw cotton acts as a depth filter in the funnel, trapping mud so only clear water passes—preventing the paper from clogging. Second, the tape manifold automatically splits the water into 4 drops. To solve chemical cross-reactivity, I pre-treated the 4 paper zones with specific pH buffers. Because Dithizone reacts selectively based on local pH, it instantly isolates each metal without mixed colours (Pink/Red for Lead, Orange for Mercury, Yellow/Brown for Copper, Purple/Red for Zinc).
